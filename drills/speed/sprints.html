Sprints

A speed drill for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Start with one foot forward, knees bent, leaning in.
  2. Push hard for the first five steps and stay leaning forward.
  3. Then run tall: knees up, arms driving from cheek to pocket.
  4. Run through the line, then rest a full minute.

Why it helps

Short, full-effort sprints with full rest let your athlete practice running at their true top speed. The first five pushing steps match how a player gets going from a standstill in a game.

The usual mistake

Kids often pop straight up on the first step and lose the lean. Watch for the body staying angled forward for those first five steps before they run tall.

Make it easier

Shorten the distance to 10 yards so every rep stays fast from start to finish.

How much to do

6 × 20 yd to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: As fast as you can, raced or timed. Full minute of rest.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: every rep at full speed, with no slowing before the line, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Monday is speed day.

Speed day. Train like a sprinter: practice how you run, then short, all-out sprints with full rest. Speed gets built when you are fresh.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
