Chase tag

A speed drill for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Mark a lane about 20 yards long.
  2. The runner starts ahead of the chaser.
  3. On "go", the runner tries to reach the end without being tagged.
  4. Switch roles every rep.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Chase tag turns sprinting into a game, so your athlete runs hard without thinking about form. Escaping a chaser and chasing someone down happen in almost every field sport and on the playground.

The usual mistake

Runners often look back over their shoulder and slow down. Tell them to keep their eyes on the end of the lane until they cross it.

Make it easier

Give them a bigger head start, three or four steps, so they get away more often.

How much to do

6 × 10 s to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: You get a two-step head start and Coach chases. Then switch.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: every rep at full speed, with no slowing before the line, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Monday is speed day.

Speed day. Train like a sprinter: practise how you run, then short, all-out sprints with full rest. Speed gets built when you are fresh.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
