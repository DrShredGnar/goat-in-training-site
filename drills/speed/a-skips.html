A-skips

A speed drill for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Mark out 15 yards. Jog down and back twice to get warm, then carioca down and back once, leading with each side.
  2. How to carioca, also called grapevine or karaoke: move sideways, crossing your back foot in front, then behind, then in front again. Your hips twist and your shoulders stay facing forward. Lead with the other side on the way back.
  3. A-skip: skip forward, lifting one knee to hip height with your toes pulled up. Then punch that foot straight down so it lands under your hips, not out in front.
  4. Stay tall. Elbows bent, hands swinging from cheek to pocket, close to your sides.
  5. Walk back after each one. Finish with two build-up runs, the second a little faster.

Why it helps

A-skips break running into one piece at a time: knee up, toe up, foot down under the hips. That same pattern shows up in every sprint, from a breakaway to a race to first base.

The usual mistake

Most kids reach the foot out in front and land on the heel. Watch for the foot coming straight down under the hips, and say "down, not out."

Make it easier

Have them march it at a walk first, then add the skip once the knee and toe look right.

How much to do

4 × 15 yd to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Jog first. Then knee up, toe up, and foot down under your hips. Walk it before you skip it.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. It starts as A-skips and becomes A-skips and wall drives; A and B skips; A, B and C skips.

When to move up: every rep at full speed, with no slowing before the line, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Monday is speed day.

Speed day. Train like a sprinter: practice how you run, then short, all-out sprints with full rest. Speed gets built when you are fresh.
