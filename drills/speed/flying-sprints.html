Flying sprints

A speed drill for ages 11 to 14. In the app it follows the session on a 45- or 60-minute weekday, and from age 12 it is part of every speed day.

How to do it

  1. Set three cones in a line: a start, one 15 yards on, and one 10 yards past that.
  2. Build up smoothly from the start, so you are at top speed by the middle cone.
  3. Run all-out from the middle cone through the last one. Stay tall and loose.
  4. Slow down gently, walk back, and rest until your breathing is easy.

Why it helps

Flying sprints let an older athlete reach top speed with a running start, so they can practice holding that speed. It matches the moment a player is already running and needs to stay fast.

The usual mistake

Kids often start too fast and are already slowing by the middle cone. Watch for a smooth build-up, so the fastest running happens in the last 10 yards.

Make it easier

Move the last cone closer so the all-out part is only about 5 yards at first.

How much to do

4 × 10 yd to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Build up for 15 yards, then 10 yards as fast as you can. Walk back and rest fully.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: every rep at full speed, with no slowing before the line, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week
