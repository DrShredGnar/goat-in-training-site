First step

A speed drill for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Stand in a ready stance: feet under your hips, one foot a little back, knees bent, chest over your toes.
  2. Coach holds a ball out to one side. Watch the ball, not Coach.
  3. The moment the ball moves, push off hard and sprint 5 yards. Keep the first step short and stay low.
  4. Walk back. Switch which foot is back on every rep.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

This drill practices reacting to something they see and getting moving in one short step. It looks like a defender jumping a pass or a shortstop breaking on a ground ball.

The usual mistake

Kids often take a big first step or stand up tall as they go. Watch for a short step and a low chest for the first few strides.

Make it easier

Start by saying "go" out loud as you move the ball, so they get a sound and a sight to react to.

How much to do

8 × 5 yd to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Go the moment Coach moves the ball. Short, quick first step.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: every rep at full speed, with no slowing before the line, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Monday is speed day.

Speed day. Train like a sprinter: practise how you run, then short, all-out sprints with full rest. Speed gets built when you are fresh.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
