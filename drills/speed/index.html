Speed drills for kids

11 speed drills a child can do in a yard, a driveway or a park, for ages 6 to 14. Short, all-out sprints with full rest, and the skipping drills sprinters use to practice how they run.

Speed day

Train like a sprinter: practice how you run, then short, all-out sprints with full rest. Speed gets built when you are fresh.

  • A-skips

    Jog first. Then knee up, toe up, and foot down under your hips. Walk it before you skip it.

  • Sprint form

    Knee up, toe up, foot punched down under your hips. Tall, slow and exact.

  • First step

    Go the moment Coach moves the ball. Short, quick first step.

  • Start races

    A different start each rep: chest down like a push-up, back, one knee, sitting.

  • Sprints

    As fast as you can, raced or timed. Full minute of rest.

  • Power skips Best from 9

    Skip for height. Big knee, big arm.

  • Chase tag

    You get a two-step head start and Coach chases. Then switch.

Harder speed drills

For athletes of 11 and up. They follow the session on a 45- or 60-minute weekday, and from age 12 they are part of every speed day.

  • Flying sprints From 11

    Build up for 15 yards, then 10 yards as fast as you can. Walk back and rest fully.

  • Sprint and stop From 11

    Stop within three steps of the line. Low and balanced.

From age 12

At 12 the speed day becomes a 45-minute session that starts with a control warm-up and adds these.

  • Falling starts From 12

    Lean until you fall into the first step, then sprint. Stay low for five steps.

Saturday practice

Everything in one practice: speed, jumps, body control, then the sport itself.

  • Reaction starts

    Go on a clap.
