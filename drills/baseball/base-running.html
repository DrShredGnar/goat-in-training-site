Base running

A baseball and softball drill from the Fielding and swings set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Mark home and first base about 20 yards apart.
  2. Sprint through first base. Do not slow down or jump at the bag.
  3. Step on the front edge of the base and look to your right for a loose ball.

Why it helps

Running through the bag trains the fastest path from home to first, which decides close plays on a routine ground ball to shortstop.

The usual mistake

Lots of kids slow down or leap at the base to touch it. Ask them to keep running full speed until they are a few steps past the bag.

Make it easier

Shorten the distance between home and first so they can focus on running through rather than speed.

How much to do

6 reps to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Home to first. Run all the way through the bag.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Baseball and softball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Fielding and swings, Throwing and Hitting and running.

Fielding and swings. Catch it, throw it, hit it. Clean and easy first, harder later.

What you need

A glove, a soft or tennis ball, a bat, and a few plastic practice balls.

Easy throws only. Stop for the day if an elbow or shoulder hurts. Before any swing, check that nobody is within two bat lengths. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
