Balance and pitch

A baseball and softball drill from the Throwing set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional at the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Stand sideways to the target, feet together.
  2. Lift your front knee and hold it for one second without wobbling.
  3. Stride straight at the target and throw.
  4. Fastballs only. Stop if your arm feels tired or sore.

Why it helps

Balance and pitch teaches a pitcher to gather on the back leg before striding, which helps them throw strikes from the mound in a game.

The usual mistake

Kids rush forward before the knee lift is steady, and the pitch drifts wide. Count "one" out loud during the hold before they stride.

Make it easier

Practice the knee lift and one-second hold without a ball first, then add easy pitches.

How much to do

10 pitches to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: From 30 feet. Balance first, then throw.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Baseball and softball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Fielding and swings, Throwing and Hitting and running.

Throwing. Good throws come from the feet up. Keep the count low and stop if the arm feels tired or sore.

What you need

A glove, a soft or tennis ball, a bat, and a few plastic practice balls.

Easy throws only. Stop for the day if an elbow or shoulder hurts. Before any swing, check that nobody is within two bat lengths. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
