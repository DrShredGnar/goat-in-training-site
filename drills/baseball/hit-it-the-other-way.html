Hit it the other way

A baseball and softball drill from the Hitting and running set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional at the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Set the ball on a tee at the outside edge of the plate, a little deeper than usual.
  2. Let the ball get deep before you swing.
  3. Keep your hands inside the ball and hit it to the opposite field.
  4. Do not pull your front shoulder out.

Why it helps

Hitting the other way trains a hitter to let an outside pitch travel and drive it to the opposite field instead of rolling over it to the shortstop.

The usual mistake

Kids try to pull everything and open the front shoulder early. Watch that the shoulder stays closed until the bat meets the ball.

Make it easier

Move the tee slightly back toward the middle of the plate until they feel the hands staying inside.

How much to do

15 swings to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Off the tee.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Baseball and softball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Fielding and swings, Throwing and Hitting and running.

Hitting and running. A level swing, a soft bunt and smart feet on the bases.

What you need

A glove, a soft or tennis ball, a bat, and a few plastic practice balls.

Easy throws only. Stop for the day if an elbow or shoulder hurts. Before any swing, check that nobody is within two bat lengths. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
