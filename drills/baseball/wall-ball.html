Wall ball

A baseball and softball drill from the Fielding and swings set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional at the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Stand about 10 feet from a solid wall with a tennis ball.
  2. Throw it low at the wall.
  3. Field the bounce with your glove out front and your feet moving.
  4. Keep it light and quick.

Why it helps

Wall ball sharpens quick hands and busy feet on short hops, the kind of tricky bounce an infielder sees when a grounder skips off the edge of the grass.

The usual mistake

Kids tend to stand flat-footed and reach with one hand. Watch for feet that keep moving and a second hand that closes over the glove.

Make it easier

Have them stand a few steps closer and throw softer at the wall so the bounce comes back slow.

How much to do

30 to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Throw and field the bounce. Two hands.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Baseball and softball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Fielding and swings, Throwing and Hitting and running.

Fielding and swings. Catch it, throw it, hit it. Clean and easy first, harder later.

What you need

A glove, a soft or tennis ball, a bat, and a few plastic practice balls.

Easy throws only. Stop for the day if an elbow or shoulder hurts. Before any swing, check that nobody is within two bat lengths. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
