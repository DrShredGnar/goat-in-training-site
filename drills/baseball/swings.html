Swings

A baseball and softball drill from the Fielding and swings set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Check that nobody is within two bat lengths of you.
  2. Stance: feet a little wider than your shoulders, knees bent, hands up by your back shoulder.
  3. Take a small step with your front foot, then turn your hips and swing level through the ball.
  4. Finish balanced, with your belly button facing where the ball went.

Why it helps

Dry swings let your athlete groove a balanced stance, step and turn without worrying about a ball, so the same motion is ready for their first at-bat on game day.

The usual mistake

Most kids swing with their arms only and fall off balance. Have them hold the finish for a count and check that the belly button faces forward.

Make it easier

Break it into pieces: stance and step first, then add the hip turn once that feels steady.

How much to do

30 swings to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: No ball. Slow and balanced: stance, step, swing, finish.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Fielding and swings. Catch it, throw it, hit it. Clean and easy first, harder later.

What you need

A glove, a soft or tennis ball, a bat, and a few plastic practice balls.

Easy throws only. Stop for the day if an elbow or shoulder hurts. Before any swing, check that nobody is within two bat lengths. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
