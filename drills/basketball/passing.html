Basketball passing

A basketball drill from the Handles and shot set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Stand facing Coach or a solid wall.
  2. Chest pass: thumbs behind the ball, step, and push it straight out. Thumbs finish pointing down.
  3. Bounce pass: same push, aimed at a spot on the ground two-thirds of the way there.
  4. Catch with both hands out in front, ready to go again.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Chest and bounce passes are the two passes a player uses most, like hitting an open teammate on the wing or passing around a defender's hands.

The usual mistake

Kids often throw with just the arms and no step, so passes float. Watch for a step toward the target and thumbs finishing down.

Make it easier

Move closer than 6 feet and let them pass without stepping until the thumb finish feels natural.

How much to do

30 chest, 30 bounce to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: From about 6 feet. Step into every pass.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Basketball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Handles and shot; Finishing and footwork; Pass, defend, rebound.

Handles and shot. Dribble with both hands and shoot with the same form every time. Eyes up, not on the ball.

What you need

A basketball that fits your hands. A hoop helps. With no hoop, shoot to a spot high on a wall.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
