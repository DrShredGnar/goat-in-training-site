Guard the ball

A basketball drill from the Pass, defend, rebound set for ages 11 to 14. In the app it follows the session on a 45- or 60-minute weekday.

How to do it

  1. Mark a lane about 10 feet wide with two cones.
  2. Coach dribbles at you and tries to get past on either side.
  3. Stay low with your arms wide and slide to keep your chest in front. Do not reach for the ball.
  4. A stop is five seconds without being passed.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Guard the ball trains a defender to keep a dribbler in front of them, the main job on the ball when an opponent tries to drive.

The usual mistake

Kids often reach for the ball and get beaten. Watch that their chest stays in front, their feet slide, and their hands stay wide rather than swiping.

Make it easier

Make the lane narrower and have Coach dribble at a walk without changing direction.

How much to do

6 stops to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Coach goes at half speed and changes direction once.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Basketball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Handles and shot; Finishing and footwork; Pass, defend, rebound.

Pass, defend, rebound. Good teams pass, guard and rebound. These are the jobs that keep you on the floor.

What you need

A basketball that fits your hands. A hoop helps. With no hoop, shoot to a spot high on a wall.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
