Other-hand layups

A basketball drill from the Finishing and footwork set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional at the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Go to the side of the hoop for your weaker hand.
  2. Step with the foot opposite your shooting hand.
  3. Lift the knee and the hand on the same side together.
  4. Use the top corner of the square.

Why it helps

Other-hand layups build a finish with the weaker hand, so a player can score on either side of the hoop when a defender takes away their strong side.

The usual mistake

Kids tend to switch back to their strong hand without noticing. Watch the hand and the knee lift together on the same side, every rep.

Make it easier

Walk through the footwork without the ball a few times, then add the ball from one step out.

How much to do

10 makes to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: From one step, no dribble.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Basketball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Handles and shot; Finishing and footwork; Pass, defend, rebound.

Finishing and footwork. Stop on balance, pivot without traveling, and finish close to the hoop with either hand.

What you need

A basketball that fits your hands. A hoop helps. With no hoop, shoot to a spot high on a wall.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
