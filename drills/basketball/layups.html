Layups

A basketball drill from the Handles and shot set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Start one step from the hoop on the right side.
  2. Step with your left foot, lift your right knee and right hand together.
  3. Lay the ball softly off the top corner of the square on the backboard.
  4. On the left side, switch everything: right foot, left knee, left hand.

Why it helps

Layups train the footwork and soft touch used on the most common close shot in a game, the finish after beating a defender to the hoop.

The usual mistake

Kids often jump off the wrong foot, or the same-side foot as their shooting hand. Say "opposite foot, same knee and hand" and check before each rep.

Make it easier

Walk through the steps without shooting first, then add the shot from one step away.

How much to do

15 each side to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: From one step, no dribble. Use the backboard.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. It starts as Layups and becomes Mikan drill.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Basketball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Handles and shot; Finishing and footwork; Pass, defend, rebound.

Handles and shot. Dribble with both hands and shoot with the same form every time. Eyes up, not on the ball.

What you need

A basketball that fits your hands. A hoop helps. With no hoop, shoot to a spot high on a wall.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
