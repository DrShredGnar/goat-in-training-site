Closeouts

A basketball drill from the Pass, defend, rebound set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Start under the hoop. Coach stands at the free-throw line with a ball.
  2. Sprint halfway, then take short choppy steps the rest of the way.
  3. Finish low with one hand up at the ball.
  4. Stay balanced. Do not jump or run past.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Closeouts teach a defender to run at a shooter under control, so they can contest a shot after help defense without getting beaten off the dribble.

The usual mistake

The common mistake is sprinting all the way and flying past Coach. Watch for the switch to short choppy steps about halfway out.

Make it easier

Shorten the distance by starting closer to the free-throw line, and let them jog the first part.

How much to do

10 reps to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Sprint, chop, hand up.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Basketball has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Handles and shot; Finishing and footwork; Pass, defend, rebound.

Pass, defend, rebound. Good teams pass, guard and rebound. These are the jobs that keep you on the floor.

What you need

A basketball that fits your hands. A hoop helps. With no hoop, shoot to a spot high on a wall.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
