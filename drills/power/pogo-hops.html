Pogo hops

A power drill, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Stand tall with your hands on your hips.
  2. Bounce on the balls of your feet with your legs nearly straight.
  3. Keep the bounces small and quick. Your heels never touch down.

Why it helps

Pogo hops practice quick, springy contacts with the ground through stiff ankles. That light bounce is part of running, skipping rope and staying on the toes in a ready stance.

The usual mistake

Kids tend to bend their knees and make big, slow jumps. Keep the bounces small and quick, and listen for quiet feet rather than thumps.

Make it easier

Do 10 bounces at a time with a short break, holding your hands for balance if needed.

How much to do

2 × 20 to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Small, quick bounces. Stiff ankles, quiet feet.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. It starts as Pogo hops and becomes Zig-zag pogos and Pogos into a sprint.

When to move up: every landing quiet and balanced with knees over toes, and every strength rep clean, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week
