Chest throws

A power drill for ages 12 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Use a light medicine ball, 4 to 8 pounds, and a solid outside wall or a partner. Check that nobody is behind you.
  2. Stand a few steps from the wall with your feet under your hips and the ball at your chest.
  3. Step forward and push the ball away as hard and as fast as you can.
  4. Let it bounce, pick it up, and reset. Every throw is a full effort.

Why it helps

Chest throws practice pushing something away fast, using the legs and arms together with a step. That push shows up in a basketball chest pass or a block in football.

The usual mistake

Kids often push with their arms only and forget the step. Watch for the front foot stepping forward as the ball leaves the chest.

Make it easier

Use a 4-pound ball and stand a step closer to the wall, focusing on the step and push.

How much to do

3 × 6 to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Ball at your chest. Step and push it away as fast as you can.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. It starts as Chest throws and becomes Overhead throws, Side throws and Scoop throws.

When to move up: every landing quiet and balanced with knees over toes, and every strength rep clean, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Wednesday is power and body control day.

From age 12. At 12 the power and body control day becomes a 45-minute session that starts with a control warm-up and adds these.

What you need

A loop resistance band and a light medicine ball, 4 to 8 pounds. In the app, only an athlete of 12 or older who has them gets this drill.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
