Body-control circuit

A power drill, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Do one exercise after another with little rest.
  2. Each move is the same version you do on Wednesday at this level.
  3. Rest two minutes between rounds.

Why it helps

The circuit strings the week's body control moves together with little rest. It practices keeping good form while tired, the way an athlete has to late in a game.

The usual mistake

Form slips as the round goes on, especially sagging hips in push-ups and bear crawls. Slowing down is fine; clean form matters more than speed.

Make it easier

Allow a short rest between moves, not only between rounds, so each one stays clean.

How much to do

3 rounds to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Squats 10, incline push-ups 8, bear crawl 10 yards, bar hang 20 seconds, one-leg balance 20 seconds each side.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: every landing quiet and balanced with knees over toes, and every strength rep clean, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Saturday is full practice, the longest session of the week.

Saturday practice. Everything in one practice: speed, jumps, body control, then the sport itself.
