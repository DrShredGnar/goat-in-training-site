Bar hang

A power drill for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Use a pull-up bar or playground monkey bars. Grab on with both hands, a little wider than your shoulders.
  2. Hang with your arms straight and your feet off the ground.
  3. Pull your shoulders down away from your ears and keep your body still.
  4. Step down onto a box or the ground. Do not drop.

Why it helps

Bar hangs practice grip and keeping the shoulders steady while holding body weight. Climbing, swinging on monkey bars and holding a bat, stick or racket all use that grip.

The usual mistake

Kids often let their shoulders shrug up toward their ears. Say "shoulders down" and watch for space between the shoulders and ears.

Make it easier

Let their toes rest lightly on the box so the arms hold only part of their weight.

How much to do

3 × 15–20 s to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Tight grip, still body.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. It starts as Bar hang and becomes Slow lowers and Pull-ups.

When to move up: every landing quiet and balanced with knees over toes, and every strength rep clean, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.
