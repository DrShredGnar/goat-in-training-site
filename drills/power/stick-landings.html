Stick landings

A power drill for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Stand on a low step, about the height of one stair.
  2. Step off. Do not jump.
  3. Land on both feet at once, as quietly as you can, knees bent and pointing over your toes.
  4. Freeze for two seconds like a statue, then stand up.

Why it helps

Stick landings practice the part of jumping kids often skip: landing quietly with knees bent. Every jump in sports ends in a landing, from a rebound to a catch in the air.

The usual mistake

Knees often cave in toward each other on landing. Watch for knees pointing over the toes, and say "knees out" if they start to drift in.

Make it easier

Use a flat floor line instead of a step, hopping gently over it to land and freeze.

How much to do

3 × 5 to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Step off a low step, land quietly, and freeze for two seconds.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. It starts as Stick landings and becomes Jump and stick, Hop and stick and One-leg hop and stick.

When to move up: every landing quiet and balanced with knees over toes, and every strength rep clean, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.
