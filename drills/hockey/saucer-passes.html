Saucer passes

A hockey drill from the Passing set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Lay a stick or a rope on the ground between you and Coach.
  2. Start with the ball on the heel of your blade.
  3. Sweep and roll your wrists so the ball slides off the toe and lifts.
  4. It should clear the stick and land flat.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

The saucer pass teaches a player to lift the ball over a stick and land it flat, the pass that gets the puck past a defender's stick to a teammate.

The usual mistake

Kids scoop the ball too high and it bounces on landing. Remind them it only needs to clear the stick and land flat.

Make it easier

Use a thinner obstacle like a rope, and stand very close so the lift can be small.

How much to do

12 passes to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: From close in. Just get it over.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Hockey has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Stickhandling, Shooting and Passing.

Passing. Flat passes onto the blade, and soft hands to catch them.

What you need

A stick cut to your height and a street-hockey or tennis ball. A net or a target on a wall you are allowed to hit.

Use a ball, not a puck, unless you have a shooting pad and a net. Keep people, pets and windows out from behind the target. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
