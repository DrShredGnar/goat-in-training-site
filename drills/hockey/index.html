Hockey drills for kids

24 hockey drills a child can do at home with a parent, for ages 6 to 14. Each one has plain steps, a starting amount and one thing to say.

What you need

A stick cut to your height and a street-hockey or tennis ball. A net or a target on a wall you are allowed to hit.

Use a ball, not a puck, unless you have a shooting pad and a net. Keep people, pets and windows out from behind the target.

Stickhandling

Soft hands, hard passes, quick shots. All of this works in shoes on a driveway.

  • Stickhandling

    Side to side in front of you. Soft, quiet touches.

  • Stickhandle on the move

    Walking. Use both sides of the blade.

  • Passing

    Forehand, from about 10 feet. Catch each one quietly.

  • Wrist shots

    From about 10 feet at a big target. Smooth, not hard.

  • Blade bounces Best from 9

    How many in a row? Try for 5.

  • Protect the ball Best from 9

    Coach reaches with a hand only.

  • Toe drags From 11

    Walking. Learn the pull with the toe of the blade.

Shooting

Quick, low and on target. Check behind your target before every shot.

  • Sweep shots

    At a wide target, along the ground.

  • Backhand shots

    Along the ground.

  • Quick release

    Pull and shoot.

  • Pick a corner

    Low corners only.

  • Shoot in stride Best from 9

    Jogging.

  • Snap shots Best from 9

    Standing still.

  • One-timers From 11

    Slow passes from close. Just meet the ball.

Passing

Flat passes onto the blade, and soft hands to catch them.

  • Wall passes

    Stop the ball each time.

  • Backhand passes

    From close in.

  • Soft hands

    Easy passes on the forehand.

  • Lead passes

    Coach walks.

  • Saucer passes Best from 9

    From close in. Just get it over.

  • One-touch passes Best from 9

    Slow and soft.

  • Give and go From 11

    Jogging. Ask for the ball with your blade down.

Saturday practice

Everything in one practice: speed, jumps, body control, then real hockey.

  • Shoot off the pass

    Stop the ball first, then shoot.

  • Cut off the angle

    Coach walks fast.

  • One-on-one moves Best from 9

    Against a cone. Fake forehand, go backhand.

The rest of a hockey week

Three of the four weekday sessions are the same for every sport: speed on Monday, power and body control on Wednesday and footwork on Thursday. Friday is the rest day: a stretch, then watch one period and draw one play. Saturday's practice ends with a game, and Sunday is play day: a different sport, or driveway hockey.
