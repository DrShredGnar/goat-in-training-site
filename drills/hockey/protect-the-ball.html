Protect the ball

A hockey drill from the Stickhandling set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Mark a square about four steps wide.
  2. Keep the ball on your stick while Coach tries to poke it away.
  3. Turn your body so you are always between Coach and the ball.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Protecting the ball teaches a player to use the body as a shield, like holding the puck along the boards while a defender reaches in.

The usual mistake

Kids keep facing Coach and leave the ball exposed. Ask them to turn their back or hip toward Coach so the ball stays on the far side.

Make it easier

Have Coach reach in slowly and only from one side at first.

How much to do

6 × 10 s to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Coach reaches with a hand only.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Hockey has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Stickhandling, Shooting and Passing.

Stickhandling. Soft hands, hard passes, quick shots. All of this works in shoes on a driveway.

What you need

A stick cut to your height and a street-hockey or tennis ball. A net or a target on a wall you are allowed to hit.

Use a ball, not a puck, unless you have a shooting pad and a net. Keep people, pets and windows out from behind the target. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
