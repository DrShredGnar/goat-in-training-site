Snap shots

A hockey drill from the Shooting set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional at the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Start with the ball at the middle of your blade, a little in front of you.
  2. Bring the blade back only a few inches.
  3. Snap it forward hard and roll your wrists.
  4. A short, quick motion. No big wind-up.

Why it helps

The snap shot trains a short, quick shot with almost no wind-up, which gives a goalie less time to react on a chance close to the net.

The usual mistake

Kids pull the blade back too far, turning it into a big wind-up. Remind them only a few inches back, then snap.

Make it easier

Let them try the motion slowly first, rolling the wrists, before adding any speed.

How much to do

15 shots to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Standing still.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Hockey has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Stickhandling, Shooting and Passing.

Shooting. Quick, low and on target. Check behind your target before every shot.

What you need

A stick cut to your height and a street-hockey or tennis ball. A net or a target on a wall you are allowed to hit.

Use a ball, not a puck, unless you have a shooting pad and a net. Keep people, pets and windows out from behind the target. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
