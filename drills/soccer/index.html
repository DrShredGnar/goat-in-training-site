Soccer drills for kids

24 soccer drills a child can do at home with a parent, for ages 6 to 14. Each one has plain steps, a starting amount and one thing to say.

What you need

A ball that is the right size for your age and a few cones or shoes. A wall helps.

No heading. Young players leave heading out of practice.

Ball control

Hundreds of touches with both feet. The ball should feel like part of your foot.

  • Toe taps and tick-tocks

    Toe taps one round, tick-tocks the next. Rest 30 seconds between.

  • Cone dribbling

    Walk, then jog. Use both feet.

  • Passing

    From about 5 yards. Stop the ball, then pass.

  • Juggling Best from 9

    Drop, one kick, catch.

  • First touch

    Easy tosses to your feet. Stop it dead with one touch.

  • Shooting

    From about 8 yards, ball standing still.

  • Touch away from pressure From 11

    Coach calls early, as the pass is made.

Passing

Firm passes along the ground with both feet, and a first touch that sets up the next one.

  • Wall passes

    Stop it, then pass.

  • Other-foot passes

    From 5 yards.

  • Through the gate

    From 5 yards.

  • Open up

    Slow passes from Coach.

  • Driven passes Best from 9

    From 10 yards.

  • One-touch passes Best from 9

    Soft passes.

  • One-two From 11

    Jogging. Five with Coach on each side.

Moves and finishing

One good move to get past a defender, then a shot on target.

  • Pull-back turns

    Walking.

  • Step-overs

    Walking. Learn the steps.

  • Inside-outside cuts

    Slow and close.

  • Dribble and shoot

    Jog in and shoot.

  • Other-foot shots Best from 9

    Ball standing still.

  • Beat the cone and shoot Best from 9

    Any move you like.

  • First-time finishes From 11

    Slow rolls onto your stronger foot.

Saturday practice

Everything in one practice: speed, jumps, body control, then real soccer.

  • One-on-one to gates

    Coach defends at walking speed.

  • Receive and turn Best from 9

    Turn with the inside of your foot.

  • Defend the line

    Coach walks with the ball.

The rest of a soccer week

Three of the four weekday sessions are the same for every sport: speed on Monday, power and body control on Wednesday and footwork on Thursday. Friday is the rest day: a stretch, then watch ten minutes and draw one play. Saturday's practice ends with a game, and Sunday is play day: a different sport, or backyard touches.
