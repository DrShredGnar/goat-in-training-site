Juggling

A soccer drill from the Ball control set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Hold the ball at waist height and drop it.
  2. Kick it up with your laces, toe pointed slightly up, to about waist height.
  3. Keep your ankle locked and the touches small.

Why it helps

Juggling builds touch and focus on the ball, which helps a player control a high ball that drops out of the air in a game.

The usual mistake

Kids kick too hard and chase the ball across the yard. Keep the touches small and the ankle locked so the ball rises only to the waist.

Make it easier

Let the ball bounce once between each kick before they catch it.

How much to do

15 each foot to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Drop, one kick, catch.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 8 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Soccer has 3 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Ball control, Passing and Moves and finishing.

Ball control. Hundreds of touches with both feet. The ball should feel like part of your foot.

What you need

A ball that is the right size for your age and a few cones or shoes. A wall helps.

No heading. Young players leave heading out of practice. An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
