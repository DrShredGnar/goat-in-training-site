One-hand catches

A football drill from the Hands set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Take soft underhand tosses.
  2. Reach out with one hand, fingers spread wide.
  3. Squeeze the ball and pull it in to your body.

Why it helps

One-hand catches teach a strong, quick squeeze for the moments when only one hand can get there, like a pass thrown a little behind a receiver.

The usual mistake

The usual mistake is fingers pressed together, so the ball bounces off. Watch for fingers spread wide and a fast pull to the chest once it touches.

Make it easier

Toss from a shorter distance and right to the hand they are using, so they can focus on the squeeze.

How much to do

8 each hand to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Soft tosses from 5 yards. Squeeze it.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 4 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye and Carry and run.

Hands. Lots of catches from lots of angles. Watch the ball all the way into your hands.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
