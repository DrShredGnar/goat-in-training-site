Grip and flick

A football drill from the Throwing set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Hold the ball with your fingers on the laces and a little space between the ball and your palm.
  2. Point your elbow at the sky and flick the ball up with your wrist.
  3. Look for a tight spin with no wobble.
  4. Catch it and go again.

Why it helps

Grip and flick isolates the fingers and wrist, the part of the throw that puts spin on the ball so a pass flies tight instead of wobbling.

The usual mistake

Kids usually squeeze the ball deep in the palm, which kills the spin. Check that you can see a little gap between the ball and the palm before each flick.

Make it easier

Let them flick it only a short way up at first, so catching it is easy and they can focus on the spin.

How much to do

25 flicks to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Lying on your back. Flick it straight up and catch it.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 4 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye and Carry and run.

Throwing. A tight spiral starts with the grip and the feet. Throw at a target every time.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
