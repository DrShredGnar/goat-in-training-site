Circle drill

A football drill from the Routes and releases set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Set five cones in a circle about 5 yards across.
  2. Run around the outside of the circle, leaning in. Keep your inside arm tight and push off your outside foot.
  3. Stay close to the cones the whole way. Do not drift wide.
  4. After two laps, break straight out of the circle toward Coach and catch a pass.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

The circle drill teaches a receiver to run fast while leaning, the way a route bends or a runner rounds a defender, then break out of the turn for the ball.

The usual mistake

Kids drift wide of the cones and slow down to stay upright. Watch for the lean coming from the ankles and short, quick steps close to the cones.

Make it easier

Make the circle bigger and let them jog it until the lean feels natural.

How much to do

3 each way to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Jog it. Lean in with small, quick steps, then break out for the catch.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 5 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye, Carry and run and Routes and releases.

Routes and releases. Get open before the ball comes: sharp breaks, quick feet off the line, and fakes a defender believes. A receiver who stays covered never gets the ball.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
