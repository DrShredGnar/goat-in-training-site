Flag pulls

A football drill from the Carry and run set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Tuck a flag, a sock or a small towel into Coach’s waistband at the hip.
  2. Coach comes at you. Get low with your feet chopping.
  3. Watch the hips, not the head or the ball.
  4. Step to the side and pull the flag. No pushing and no grabbing clothes.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Flag pulls practice the moment a defender closes in on a runner in flag football: get low, watch the hips and take the flag, with no contact.

The usual mistake

Kids often lunge at the flag while running full speed and miss. Watch for short, chopping steps, eyes on the hips, and a step to the side before reaching.

Make it easier

Have Coach walk slowly and in a straight line, so the athlete can practice the timing of the pull.

How much to do

10 pulls to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Coach walks straight at you.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 5 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye, Carry and run and Routes and releases.

Carry and run. Hold the ball tight, make one sharp cut, and get the flag.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
