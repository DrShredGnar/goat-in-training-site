Slide in the pocket

A football drill from the Throwing set for ages 6 to 14.

How to do it

  1. Face Coach with the ball in two hands, high at your chest, like a quarterback in the pocket.
  2. Coach points left or right. Slide two quick steps that way without crossing your feet, eyes on Coach.
  3. Stop, set your feet, and turn your front shoulder to Coach.
  4. Step at Coach with your front foot, then throw. Your back foot comes through after the ball is gone.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

When the rush closes in, a quarterback slides a step or two to a clean spot inside the pocket, then sets the feet and steps into the throw. Throwing while still moving is how passes sail.

The usual mistake

Kids often cross their feet on the slide, or throw before they stop. Watch for short, quick steps with the feet apart, then a clear stop, the front shoulder at Coach, and "step, then throw."

Make it easier

Leave the slide out at first: face Coach, turn, step and throw until the order is smooth. Then add one slide step, then two.

How much to do

15 throws to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: From 5 yards. Slide, stop, step, throw.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 4 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye and Carry and run.

Throwing. A tight spiral starts with the grip and the feet. Throw at a target every time.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
