Football drills for kids

31 football drills a child can do at home with a parent, for ages 6 to 14. Each one has plain steps, a starting amount and one thing to say.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

Hands

Lots of catches from lots of angles. Watch the ball all the way into your hands.

  • Tennis ball wall catches

    Throw at a wall and catch with one hand. Soft fingers, eyes on the ball.

  • High catches

    From 5 yards. Thumbs together, hands make a diamond.

  • Low catches

    From 5 yards. Pinkies together, bend your knees, scoop.

  • Over the shoulder Best from 9

    Jogging. Let the ball drop into your hands.

  • One-hand catches Best from 9

    Soft tosses from 5 yards. Squeeze it.

  • Catch and tuck

    Catch, tuck high and tight, and run past Coach, who swats at the ball.

  • Sideline catches From 11

    Jog to the line. Catch, then tap both feet down inside it.

Throwing

A tight spiral starts with the grip and the feet. Throw at a target every time.

  • Grip and flick

    Lying on your back. Flick it straight up and catch it.

  • One-knee throws

    From 5 yards. Slow and smooth.

  • Step and throw

    From 5 yards. Step first, then throw.

  • Target throws

    From 5 yards.

  • Throw on the move Best from 9

    Jogging, 5 yards from Coach.

  • Three-step drop Best from 9

    Walk the steps first, then throw to Coach 5 yards away.

  • Slide and throw From 11

    Drop, slide two steps, set your feet, and throw to Coach 8 yards away.

Hand-eye

Find the ball late and catch it with your hands. Make practice harder than a game, so the game feels easy.

  • Drop catches

    Catch it after one bounce.

  • Wall ball from behind

    Underhand throws. Let it bounce once, then catch it.

  • Around the post

    Soft throws from 4 yards, right at your hands.

  • Pump and catch

    From 5 yards. Coach throws on ten.

  • One-eye catches Best from 9

    Underhand tosses from 4 yards with a tennis ball or a soft ball.

  • Two-ball catches Best from 9

    Soft tosses from three steps away, both at chest height.

  • Blind turn catches From 11

    Coach shouts early. Turn the same way every time.

Carry and run

Hold the ball tight, make one sharp cut, and get the flag.

  • Handoffs

    Walking. Four from each side.

  • Plant and cut

    Jog in, sharp cut out.

  • Hold on tight

    Coach swats once.

  • Flag pulls

    Coach walks straight at you.

  • Switch hands Best from 9

    Walking.

  • Catch and go Best from 9

    Catch, tuck, turn, go.

  • Read and cut From 11

    Coach steps early. Jog in and make one sharp cut.

Saturday practice

Everything in one practice: speed, jumps, body control, then real football.

  • Route tree

    Hitch, slant, out and go. Sharp cut, head snaps around, hands ready.

  • Stance and get-off Best from 9

    Fire out the moment Coach moves the ball.

  • Flag pursuit

    Coach jogs.

The rest of a football week

Three of the four weekday sessions are the same for every sport: speed on Monday, power and body control on Wednesday and footwork on Thursday. Friday is the rest day: a stretch, then watch one drive and draw one play. Saturday's practice ends with a game, and Sunday is play day: a different sport, or backyard catch.
