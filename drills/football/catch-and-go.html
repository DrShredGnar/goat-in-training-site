Catch and go

A football drill from the Carry and run set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional on the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Stand five yards from Coach and face the throw.
  2. Catch a short pass with your hands, not your body.
  3. Tuck it, turn, and sprint ten yards.
  4. Turn the same way every rep, then switch.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Catch and go connects a short catch to turning upfield, which is what a receiver does after a quick pass to gain extra yards.

The usual mistake

Kids often turn to run before the catch is finished. Remind them to watch the ball in, tuck it, and only then turn and sprint.

Make it easier

Shorten the sprint to five yards and let them jog it until the catch, tuck and turn feel smooth.

How much to do

10 catches to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Catch, tuck, turn, go.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 4 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye and Carry and run.

Carry and run. Hold the ball tight, make one sharp cut, and get the flag.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
