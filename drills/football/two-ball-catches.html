Two-ball catches

A football drill from the Hand-eye set, best from age 9. For a younger athlete it is the last drill of the session, and optional at the weekend.

How to do it

  1. Coach holds two tennis balls in one hand and tosses them together, underhand.
  2. Catch one in each hand.
  3. Keep your head still and see both. Do not turn to follow just one.

"Coach" is whoever is helping: a parent, an older brother or sister, or a friend.

Why it helps

Two-ball catches train wide vision and calm hands, which helps a player see the whole field instead of locking on one thing.

The usual mistake

The common mistake is turning the head to chase one ball and missing the other. Watch that their head stays still and both hands move together.

Make it easier

Start with a toss of one ball to each hand, one after the other, before tossing both together.

How much to do

10 tosses to start, at the Rookie level.

What to say: Soft tosses from three steps away, both at chest height.

How it gets harder

The app has four levels of this drill: Rookie, Starter, Captain and All-Star. The move stays the same and the amount, the distance or the speed goes up.

When to move up: at least 9 clean reps out of 10 in every drill, for two straight weeks. A parent decides.

Where it fits in the week

Tuesday is skill day. Football has 4 sets of skill drills and uses one a week: Hands, Throwing, Hand-eye and Carry and run.

Hand-eye. Find the ball late and catch it with your hands. Make practice harder than a game, so the game feels easy.

What you need

A football and two tennis balls. A flag or a small towel to pull, and a hoop, box or marked spot to throw at.

An adult should be there for every session, and stop if anything hurts. This is general training guidance, not medical advice: the Terms of Use have the full safety notes.
